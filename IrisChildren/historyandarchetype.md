# 1. Ancient Civilizations and the Suppression Game

## 1.1. Imperial China — The Scholar’s Ceiling
In many dynasties, scholars were invited to serve the state, but:
- if they rose too quickly → threat,
- if they gained influence → insult to superiors,
- if they became useful → removed.

The imperial exam system created **false ladders**:
- rise looked possible,
- but was tightly controlled.

The Iris Children here were the **wandering sages**, the ones who practiced wu wei to avoid political destruction.

---

## 1.2. Rome — The Patronage Trap
Roman society ran on patron–client networks:
- patrons offered opportunities,
- but punished any rise that bypassed them.

Contacts were given, then withdrawn.  
Contracts offered, then revoked.

The Iris Children here were the **Stoics**, who learned:
> “You cannot control the hierarchy, only your inner state.”

---

## 1.3. Medieval Europe — The Courtier’s Paradox
Courts invited talented people:
- poets,
- scholars,
- strategists,
- engineers.

But if their talent threatened the nobility, they were:
- exiled,
- silenced,
- or absorbed into powerless roles.

The Iris Children here were the **Hermeticists**, who hid knowledge in symbols to avoid punishment.
