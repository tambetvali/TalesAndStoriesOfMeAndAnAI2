# 1. Historic Motives & Classic Games

## The “Work–Obligation” Game  
Throughout history, societies have struggled with the question:  
**Does suffering create obligation?**  
This appears in:

- **Feudalism** — peasants believed their toil bound lords to protect them.  
- **Religious asceticism** — suffering as proof of devotion.  
- **Industrial labor movements** — workers demanding recognition for invisible labor.  
- **Modern gig economy** — blurred lines between autonomy and exploitation.

Losel reenacts this ancient pattern:  
the belief that *pain itself* creates a contract.

### **[Social Contract Theory](ca://s?q=Explain_social_contract_theory)**  
Vice’s stance echoes philosophers who argued that obligation arises from **agreement**, not **suffering**.  
Key thinkers:  
- Thomas Hobbes  
- John Locke  
- Jean‑Jacques Rousseau  

Losel represents the *pre‑contract* worldview:  
a world where survival = legitimacy.

# 3. Literature & Authors to Study

## 3.1. Psychology & Human Behavior  
These authors explore the dynamics of survival, meaning, and misunderstanding:

- **Abraham Maslow** — hierarchy of needs  
- **Viktor Frankl** — meaning under suffering  
- **Erving Goffman** — social roles and masks  
- **Daniel Kahneman** — cognitive biases  
- **Carl Jung** — archetypes and the collective unconscious  

Losel’s worldview is a case study in **survival cognition**.  
Vice’s worldview is **self‑actualization**.  
Iris navigates the middle.

---

## 3.2. Game Theory & Strategy  
To understand the “who works for whom” paradox:

- **John von Neumann & Oskar Morgenstern** — foundational game theory  
- **Thomas Schelling** — coordination and conflict  
- **Robert Axelrod** — cooperation and reciprocity  
- **Elinor Ostrom** — commons and shared responsibility  

The trio plays incompatible games:  
- Losel: zero‑sum  
- Iris: cooperative  
- Vice: meta‑strategic

---

## 3.3. Philosophy & Meaning  
Vice’s language echoes:

- **Ludwig Wittgenstein** — meaning as use  
- **Immanuel Kant** — intention as moral foundation  
- **Hannah Arendt** — labor vs. work vs. action  
- **Simone Weil** — attention and obligation  
- **Søren Kierkegaard** — subjective truth  

These thinkers help decode Vice’s insistence on **directionality** and **intention**.

## 3.4. Spirituality & Myth  
The inverted Yggdrasil and the spiritual tension draw from:

- **Tao Te Ching** — wu wei, non‑forcing  
- **Zen koans** — paradox as clarity  
- **Norse mythology** — world‑tree, warrior logic

Iris embodies **compassionate Taoism**.

Losel embodies **the warrior who cannot stop fighting**.
